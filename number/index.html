Junior Cycle Mathematics

Number Strand

Number is about representing quantities clearly, calculating accurately, recognising equivalent forms, reasoning proportionally, analysing patterns and working with sets.

Use this page as a map. Each learning outcome is a main stopping point. The detail underneath can be explored in smaller pieces rather than read all at once.

N.1 — Numbers and arithmetic operations

Investigate the representation of numbers and arithmetic operations so that students can:

  1. represent addition, subtraction, multiplication and division in \(\mathbb N\), \(\mathbb Z\) and \(\mathbb Q\), using models including the number line, decomposition and accumulating groups of equal size
  2. perform the four operations and understand the commutative, associative and distributive properties, including work with surds
  3. explore numbers in index form and use index laws, including the order of operations and brackets
  4. calculate and interpret factors, highest common factor, multiples, lowest common multiple and prime numbers
  5. present numerical answers to a specified degree of accuracy
  6. convert decimal numbers to standard form \(a\times10^n\), where appropriate

Examples of the notation used here include \(2^5=32\), \(\sqrt{12}=2\sqrt3\), and \(4.7\times10^6\). Click a typeset expression to enlarge it.

N.2 — Equivalent forms of rational numbers

Investigate equivalent representations of rational numbers so that students can:

  1. convert flexibly between fractions, decimals and percentages
  2. use and understand ratio and proportion
  3. solve money-related problems including bills, VAT, profit or loss, compound interest, income tax, net pay, value for money, mark-up and margin

For example, \(\frac34=0.75=75\%\). These are different representations of the same rational number.

N.3 — Proportionality

Investigate situations involving proportionality so that students can:

  1. use absolute and relative comparison where appropriate
  2. solve problems involving proportionality, including currency conversion and average speed, distance and time

A useful relationship is \(\text{average speed}=\dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}\).

N.4 — Numerical patterns

Analyse numerical patterns in different ways, including tables and graphs, and continue such patterns.

For example, \(3,7,11,15,\ldots\) can be represented numerically, in a table, on a graph and eventually by a general rule.

N.5 — Sets

Explore the concept of a set so that students can:

  1. understand sets and set equality
  2. define sets by listing elements or by generating rules
  3. use set notation including \(\varnothing\), \(\subset\), \(\in\), \(\cap\), \(\cup\), \(\backslash\), \(\mathbb N\), \(\mathbb Z\), \(\mathbb Q\) and \(\mathbb R\)
  4. perform union, intersection, difference and complement operations
  5. investigate whether set operations are commutative and/or associative

If \(A=\{1,2,3\}\) and \(B=\{3,4\}\), then \(A\cap B=\{3\}\) and \(A\cup B=\{1,2,3,4\}\).
