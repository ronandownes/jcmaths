Expectations for Students

Learning outcomes describe what knowledge, understanding, skills and values students should be able to demonstrate having studied mathematics in junior cycle.

Junior cycle mathematics is offered at Ordinary and Higher level. The majority of learning outcomes apply to all students; additional Higher-level learning outcomes are highlighted in bold in the source specification.

The learning outcomes represent outcomes for students at the end of their three years of study. They are not intended to be treated as isolated items that are “completed” once and then left behind.

Unifying strand

Unifying strand

U.1
Recall and demonstrate understanding of the fundamental concepts and procedures that underpin each strand.
U.2
Apply the procedures associated with each strand accurately, effectively and appropriately.
U.3
Recognise that equality is a relationship in which two mathematical expressions have the same value.
U.4
Represent a mathematical situation in a variety of different ways, including numerically, algebraically, graphically, physically and in words; and interpret, analyse and compare such representations.
U.5
Make connections within and between strands.
U.6
Make connections between mathematics and the real world.
U.7
Make sense of a given problem and, if necessary, mathematise a situation.
U.8
Apply knowledge and skills to solve a problem, including decomposing it into manageable parts and/or simplifying it using appropriate assumptions.
U.9
Interpret a solution to a problem in terms of the original question.
U.10
Evaluate different possible solutions to a problem, including reasonableness, improvements and limitations.
U.11
Generate general mathematical statements or conjectures based on specific instances.
U.12
Generate and evaluate mathematical arguments and proofs.
U.13
Communicate mathematics effectively: justify reasoning, interpret results, explain conclusions, and use mathematical language and notation precisely.

Number strand

Number strand

N.1

Investigate the representation of numbers and arithmetic operations so that students can:

  1. represent addition, subtraction, multiplication and division in \(\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q\) using models including the number line, decomposition and accumulating groups of equal size
  2. perform the four operations and understand commutative, associative and distributive properties, including work with surds
  3. explore numbers in index form and use index laws, including order of operations and brackets
  4. calculate and interpret factors, highest common factor, multiples, lowest common multiple and prime numbers
  5. present numerical answers to a specified degree of accuracy
  6. convert decimal numbers to standard form \(a\times10^n\), where appropriate
N.2

Investigate equivalent representations of rational numbers so that students can:

  1. convert flexibly between fractions, decimals and percentages
  2. use and understand ratio and proportion
  3. solve money-related problems including bills, VAT, profit or loss, compound interest, income tax, net pay, value for money, mark-up and margin
N.3

Investigate situations involving proportionality so that students can:

  1. use absolute and relative comparison where appropriate
  2. solve problems involving proportionality, including currency conversion and average speed, distance and time
N.4
Analyse numerical patterns in different ways, including tables and graphs, and continue such patterns.
N.5

Explore the concept of a set so that students can:

  1. understand sets and set equality
  2. define sets by listing elements or by generating rules
  3. use set notation including \(\varnothing,\subset,\in,\cap,\cup,\backslash,\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R\)
  4. perform union, intersection, difference and complement operations
  5. investigate whether set operations are commutative and/or associative

Geometry and trigonometry

Geometry and trigonometry strand

GT.1
Calculate, interpret and apply units of measure and time.
GT.2
Investigate 2D shapes and 3D solids, including scaled diagrams, nets, perimeter, area, volume and surface area.
GT.3
Investigate the concept of proof through geometry, including prescribed constructions, axioms, theorems, corollaries, converses and geometrical proofs.
GT.4
Evaluate and use trigonometric ratios \(\sin\), \(\cos\) and \(\tan\), and their inverses, for right-angled triangles.
GT.5
Investigate points, lines and line segments in the coordinate plane, including distance, midpoint, slope, intersection, parallel and perpendicular lines, and equations of lines.
GT.6
Investigate transformations of simple objects, including translation, central symmetry, axial symmetry and rotation.

Algebra and functions

Algebra and functions strand

AF.1
Investigate patterns and relationships — linear, quadratic, doubling and tripling — and represent them in tables, graphs, words and algebraic expressions.
AF.2
Investigate situations in which letters stand for variable quantities; generate and interpret expressions and equations.
AF.3
Apply arithmetic properties and factorisation to generate equivalent expressions, including simplifying, multiplying, dividing and moving flexibly between factorised and expanded forms.
AF.4
Select and use suitable strategies — graphical, numeric, algebraic, trial and improvement, and working backwards — for linear equations, quadratic equations, simultaneous equations and linear inequalities.
AF.5
Generate quadratic equations given integer roots.
AF.6
Apply the relationship between operations and order of operations to change the subject of a formula.
AF.7
Investigate functions; represent and interpret them graphically, diagrammatically, verbally and algebraically; use function notation; solve equations and inequalities graphically; and connect graph shape with phenomena.

Statistics and probability

Statistics and probability strand

SP.1
Investigate outcomes of experiments, generate sample spaces systematically, and use the fundamental principle of counting.
SP.2
Investigate random events, understand probability on a 0–1 scale, calculate probability for equally likely outcomes, and use relative frequency as an estimate of probability.
SP.3
Carry out a statistical investigation: generate a question; plan and collect unbiased, representative data; classify data; select and interpret displays; calculate and interpret summary statistics; evaluate displays; discuss misuse of statistics; and discuss assumptions and limitations of conclusions.
