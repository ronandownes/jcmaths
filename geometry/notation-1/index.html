Points and Lines

Take a fixed plane and consider points and lines that lie in it. The plane and the lines are sets of points. Each line is a subset of the plane: every point on the line is also a point of the plane.

Build your own: move the two points anywhere in the plane. Then drag the line itself. The line moves independently of the points.

Try making one point lie on the line while the other point stays off it.

Notation 1

We denote points by Roman capital letters A, B, C, etc., and lines by lower-case Roman letters l, m, n, etc.

point → capital · line → lower-case

What to notice

A line is a set of points, and it is a subset of the plane.

Each line is endless, extending forever in both directions, and each line has infinitely many points.

The two coloured points are not being used to define the line. They and the line can be moved independently. A point may lie on the line, or it may lie elsewhere in the plane.

Key language: a point is an element of the plane; a line is a set of points and a subset of the plane.
