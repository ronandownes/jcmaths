Junior Cycle Mathematics

Algebra

This page presents the Algebra learning outcomes from the combined Algebra and Functions strand. Each learning outcome is an H2 and therefore feeds the Algebra dropdown automatically.

Keep the structure visible. Read the learning outcome first, then use the examples and activities only when they help.

AF.1 — Patterns and relationships

Investigate patterns and relationships — linear, quadratic, doubling and tripling — and represent them in tables, graphs, words and algebraic expressions.

For example, \(3,7,11,15,\ldots\) can be connected to a rule such as \(4n-1\).

AF.2 — Variables, expressions and equations

Investigate situations in which letters stand for variable quantities; generate and interpret expressions and equations.

If a taxi costs €4 plus €2 per kilometre, the cost after \(x\) kilometres can be represented by \(4+2x\).

AF.3 — Equivalent expressions and factorisation

Apply arithmetic properties and factorisation to generate equivalent expressions, including simplifying, multiplying, dividing and moving flexibly between factorised and expanded forms.

For example, \(3x+2x=5x\) and \(3(x+4)=3x+12\).

Interactive: Simplify Expressions practises collecting like terms from simple repeated variables through mixed expressions and powers.

AF.4 — Solving equations and inequalities

Select and use suitable strategies — graphical, numeric, algebraic, trial and improvement, and working backwards — for linear equations, quadratic equations, simultaneous equations and linear inequalities.

A linear equation such as \(3x+5=20\) can be solved algebraically, but its solution can also be interpreted graphically.

AF.5 — Generate quadratic equations

Generate quadratic equations given integer roots.

If the roots are \(2\) and \(-3\), then \((x-2)(x+3)=0\), giving \(x^2+x-6=0\).

AF.6 — Change the subject of a formula

Apply the relationship between operations and order of operations to change the subject of a formula.

From \(v=u+at\), subtracting \(u\) and dividing by \(a\) gives \(t=\dfrac{v-u}{a}\).
