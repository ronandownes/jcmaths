The Theory

The Theory

Geometry refers to plane geometry. Points are denoted by capital letters \(A,B,C\), and lines by lower-case letters \(l,m,n\).

Axiom 1 — Two Points Axiom

There is exactly one line through any two given points.

Definition 1 — Segment and ray

The line segment \([AB]\) is the part of the line \(AB\) between \(A\) and \(B\), including the endpoints. The ray that starts at \(A\) and passes through \(B\) is denoted \([AB\).

Definition 2 — Collinear

If three or more points lie on a single line, they are collinear.

Definition 3 — Triangle

For non-collinear points \(A,B,C\), the triangle \(\triangle ABC\) is the piece of the plane enclosed by the three line segments \([AB]\), \([BC]\) and \([CA]\).

Definition 4 — Distance

The distance between points \(A\) and \(B\) is denoted \(|AB|\). The length of segment \([AB]\) is \(|AB|\).

Constructions

Constructions to Study

  1. Bisector of a given angle, using compass and straight edge.
  2. Perpendicular bisector of a segment, using compass and straight edge.
  3. Line perpendicular to a given line, through a point not on the line.
  4. Line perpendicular to a given line, through a point on the line.
  5. Line parallel to a given line, through a given point.
  6. Division of a segment into 2 or 3 equal segments without measuring.
  7. Division of a segment into any number of equal segments without measuring.
  8. Line segment of given length on a given ray.
  9. Angle of given number of degrees with a given ray as one arm.
  10. Triangle given three side lengths.
  11. Triangle given SAS data.
  12. Triangle given ASA data.
  13. Right-angled triangle given the hypotenuse and one other side.
  14. Right-angled triangle given one side and one acute angle.
  15. Rectangle given side lengths.
  16. Circumcentre and circumcircle of a triangle.
  17. Incentre and incircle of a triangle.
  18. Angle of 60° without a protractor or set square.
  19. Tangent to a circle at a given point.
  20. Parallelogram given side lengths and angles.
  21. Centroid of a triangle.
  22. Orthocentre of a triangle.

JCOL Content

Junior Cycle content

Ordinary Level

Students study the prescribed concepts, constructions 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 and 15, and are exposed to the language of theorem, proof, axiom, corollary, converse and implies.

They see Axioms 1–5 and study proofs of Theorems 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9, 10, 14 and 15, together with direct proofs of Corollaries 3 and 4. The statement and use of Theorem 13 are studied, but its formal proof is not required.

Additional Higher Level content

Higher Level adds the concept of concurrent lines and constructions 3 and 7. Students study proofs of Theorems 13 and 19, Corollaries 1–5 and their converses. They use Theorems 11 and 12 without needing to study their formal proofs.
